# Chief Human Resources Officer (CHRO) - Detailed Role Description

**Classification:** Internal - Confidential  
**Last Updated:** February 2026  
**Organization:** Artifact Virtual (SMC-Private) Limited

---

## Position Summary

The Chief Human Resources Officer is the architect of Artifact Virtual's people strategy, responsible for building a world-class technical organization that can compete globally for AI/ML talent while operating efficiently in emerging markets. This role demands a unique combination of technical recruiting expertise, compensation design mastery, cultural leadership, and international HR management to scale from a founding team to a 100+ person organization across multiple geographies.

---

## Day-to-Day Responsibilities

### Morning Routine (7:00 AM - 10:00 AM)

**People Metrics Review (30 minutes)**
- Review recruiting pipeline: active candidates, interviews scheduled, offers extended, acceptances
- Check employee engagement metrics: recent survey results, pulse check responses, sentiment analysis
- Monitor attrition alerts: resignation notices, flight risk indicators, engagement score drops
- Review time-to-hire and time-to-fill metrics for open positions
- Scan employment law updates and compliance alerts

**Recruiting & Talent Pipeline Management (60 minutes)**
- Review candidates for critical open roles (senior engineers, engineering managers, technical leads)
- Prepare for candidate interviews and hiring manager debriefs
- Coordinate with recruiting team on sourcing strategies and outreach campaigns
- Review compensation proposals and approve offer packages
- Follow up on outstanding offers and negotiate with candidates

**Executive Team Sync (30 minutes)**
- Daily or weekly standup with CEO and C-suite on people matters
- Address immediate people blockers: urgent hiring needs, employee issues, compensation questions
- Align on day's critical people decisions: offers, promotions, organizational changes
- Review progress on people OKRs and key hiring milestones

**Strategic Thinking Time (60 minutes)**
- Deep work on people strategy initiatives: compensation redesign, organizational restructures, culture programs
- Long-term workforce planning and headcount modeling
- Competitive intelligence: market salary data, talent availability, competitor hiring
- Innovation in HR practices and talent strategies

### Midday Activities (10:00 AM - 2:00 PM)

**Candidate Interviews (Variable)**
- Interview candidates for senior technical roles (Staff Engineer+, Engineering Managers, Directors)
- Interview candidates for HR team roles (recruiters, HR operations, L&D specialists)
- Conduct executive interviews for VP and C-suite positions
- Participate in final round interviews to assess culture fit and leadership potential

**Hiring Manager Partnerships (Variable)**
- Partner with engineering managers on hiring strategies for their teams
- Conduct intake meetings for new job openings: role requirements, candidate profiles, compensation ranges
- Train hiring managers on interviewing best practices and reducing bias
- Review hiring decisions and participate in offer approval meetings
- Coach managers on recruitment marketing and employer branding

**Compensation & Equity Management (Ongoing)**
- Review and approve offer letters and compensation packages
- Design salary bands and equity grants for new roles
- Conduct compensation benchmarking against market data
- Process equity grants and manage stock option administration
- Review promotion and raise requests from managers

### Afternoon Focus (2:00 PM - 6:00 PM)

**Performance Management & Employee Development (Variable)**
- Conduct 1-on-1 meetings with HR direct reports (recruiters, HR ops, L&D managers)
- Coach managers on performance issues and difficult conversations
- Review performance improvement plans (PIPs) and termination decisions
- Facilitate calibration sessions for performance reviews
- Approve promotion recommendations and advancement decisions

**Culture & Engagement Initiatives (Variable)**
- Plan and execute culture-building events: town halls, team offsites, recognition programs
- Review employee engagement survey results and develop action plans
- Meet with employee resource groups (ERGs) and diversity initiatives
- Address employee relations issues: conflicts, complaints, concerns
- Conduct skip-level meetings with high-potential employees and flight-risk talent

**Organizational Design & Workforce Planning (Variable)**
- Design organizational structures for new departments or teams
- Plan headcount additions and budget allocation across departments
- Create role definitions and career frameworks for new positions
- Model workforce scenarios: growth projections, budget constraints, hiring timelines
- Review and optimize organizational efficiency metrics

### Evening Work (6:00 PM - 9:00 PM)

**International Coordination (Variable)**
- Meet with US/EU candidates and employees (time zone accommodation)
- Coordinate with international HR partners and EOR services
- Review international employment contracts and compensation packages
- Manage international compliance and legal requirements

**Communication & Stakeholder Management**
- Email and communication catch-up with candidates, employees, managers
- Prepare board updates on people metrics and talent strategy
- Update CEO on critical people issues and hiring progress
- Review HR policy documentation and employee handbook updates

**Continuous Learning**
- Stay current on HR trends, employment law changes, compensation benchmarks
- Research best practices in technical recruiting and culture scaling
- Read industry reports on AI/ML talent markets and engineering culture
- Network with other CHROs and HR leaders in tech sector

### Weekly Cadence

**Monday: Strategic Planning & Recruiting**
- Week kickoff with HR team: review open roles, recruiting priorities, key hires
- Review weekly hiring goals and metrics: interviews scheduled, offers extended, start dates
- Strategic workforce planning session: align headcount with business priorities
- Recruiting pipeline review: funnel metrics, conversion rates, sourcing effectiveness

**Tuesday: Talent & Performance**
- 1-on-1 meetings with direct reports (rotating weekly)
- Review performance issues and PIPs with managers
- Coaching sessions for managers on leadership development
- Calibration discussions for promotions and compensation adjustments

**Wednesday: Compensation & Benefits**
- Compensation committee work: review market data, adjust salary bands, approve equity grants
- Benefits administration review: enrollment, claims, vendor management
- Equity program management: option grants, exercise windows, vesting schedules
- Total rewards strategy development and refinement

**Thursday: Culture & Engagement**
- Employee engagement initiatives: survey reviews, action planning, program launches
- Meet with ERGs and diversity initiative leaders
- Plan culture events and team-building activities
- Address employee relations issues and conflict resolution

**Friday: Compliance & Administration**
- HR compliance review: employment law updates, policy audits, documentation checks
- HRIS and HR technology administration: data accuracy, system optimization
- Prepare board materials and executive updates on people metrics
- Week retrospective with HR team: wins, challenges, lessons learned, next week priorities

### Monthly Activities

**First Week: Strategic Reviews**
- Monthly business review: present people metrics to executive team
- Recruiting performance review: monthly hiring goals vs. actuals, pipeline health
- Headcount and budget review: actual vs. plan, forecast for upcoming months
- Talent review: high performers, high potentials, flight risk, succession planning

**Second Week: Compensation & Benefits**
- Compensation benchmarking: review latest market data, adjust offers and bands
- Benefits review: utilization, cost, employee satisfaction, vendor performance
- Equity administration: monthly grants, exercises, terminations
- International compensation: currency adjustments, cost-of-living reviews

**Third Week: Performance & Development**
- Performance management: review cycle status, coaching needs, PIP progress
- Learning & development: program effectiveness, budget utilization, training requests
- Leadership development: pipeline review, high-potential employee development plans
- Career framework updates: level definitions, promotion criteria, skill requirements

**Fourth Week: Culture & Engagement**
- Employee engagement: survey administration, results analysis, action planning
- Culture initiatives: program launches, event planning, recognition
- DEI initiatives: diversity metrics, inclusive hiring reviews, ERG support
- People strategy refinement: OKR progress, initiative adjustments, long-term planning

### Quarterly Activities

**Quarterly Business Reviews**
- Present comprehensive people update to board: headcount, attrition, engagement, diversity
- Review quarterly OKRs with CEO: achievement rates, adjustments, next quarter priorities
- Conduct quarterly all-hands participation: people updates, Q&A, culture building
- Quarterly talent review: succession planning, organizational health, leadership bench strength

**Quarterly People Processes**
- Performance review cycles: coordinate reviews, calibrations, feedback delivery, ratings
- Compensation review cycles: merit increases, promotion adjustments, equity refresh grants
- Engagement surveys: quarterly pulse surveys, annual comprehensive survey
- Learning & development: quarterly training calendars, conference sponsorships, certification programs

## Reporting Structure

### Direct Reports

1. **VP Talent Acquisition** (Hired Year 1-2)
   - Leads all technical and non-technical recruiting efforts
   - Manages recruiting team: technical recruiters, university recruiting coordinator
   - Owns recruiting metrics, pipeline management, employer branding
   - Reports on time-to-hire, cost-per-hire, quality-of-hire, diversity hiring

2. **HR Operations Manager** (Hired Year 1)
   - Manages day-to-day HR operations: onboarding, offboarding, benefits, compliance
   - Owns HRIS administration and HR technology systems
   - Leads HR compliance and employment law adherence
   - Manages payroll coordination, leave administration, employee records

3. **Learning & Development Manager** (Hired Year 2)
   - Designs and delivers technical training and leadership development programs
   - Manages L&D budget and vendor relationships (Udemy, Coursera, training providers)
   - Coordinates conference sponsorships and certification programs
   - Owns onboarding excellence and manager training

4. **Compensation & Benefits Specialist** (Hired Year 2-3)
   - Manages compensation benchmarking, salary bands, and equity programs
   - Administers benefits programs and vendor relationships
   - Coordinates performance reviews and merit increase cycles
   - Conducts pay equity analyses

5. **Employee Relations Specialist** (Hired Year 3)
   - Handles employee relations issues, conflicts, and complaints
   - Manages performance improvement plans and terminations
   - Conducts investigations and ensures fair treatment
   - Coordinates employee engagement initiatives

### Indirect Reports
- Technical Recruiters (2-3 by Year 3)
- HR Coordinator / HR Generalist (1-2 by Year 3)
- Total HR team size: 6-8 people by Year 3

### Reports To
- **Chief Executive Officer (CEO)** - Primary reporting relationship, strategic partnership
- **Board of Directors** - Quarterly updates on people strategy, metrics, and organizational health
- **Compensation Committee** (Board subcommittee) - Annual compensation review, equity programs

### Key Partnerships
- **Chief Financial Officer (CFO)** - Headcount planning, compensation budgets, financial modeling
- **Chief Technology Officer (CTO)** - Technical hiring, engineering culture, organizational design for technical teams
- **Chief Operating Officer (COO)** - Operations team hiring, performance management, organizational effectiveness
- **General Counsel** - Employment law compliance, contracts, litigation, policy development
- **All Department Heads** - Hiring needs, performance management, team development

## Decision-Making Authority

### Full Authority (No Approval Required)
- Candidate offers up to Director level (within approved compensation bands)
- Performance management decisions: PIPs, terminations (in consultation with legal)
- HR policy creation and updates (within legal compliance)
- Employee relations resolutions and conflict mediation
- L&D program approvals and training expenditures (within budget)
- HR vendor selection for contracts <$50K annually
- Recruiting process design and candidate experience optimization
- Day-to-day HR operations and team management
- Employee engagement initiatives and culture programs

### Requires CEO Approval
- VP and C-suite offers and compensation packages
- Organizational restructures affecting multiple departments
- Major compensation program changes (equity program redesign, salary band overhauls)
- Headcount additions beyond approved plan
- HR technology investments >$50K annually
- Significant policy changes affecting all employees (remote work policy, PTO policy)
- Executive terminations and severance packages
- Major culture initiatives requiring significant budget

### Requires Board Approval
- Annual compensation philosophy and merit increase budget
- Equity pool increases and stock option plan amendments
- Executive compensation packages (C-suite)
- Major organizational restructures (layoffs, significant reductions)
- Changes to employee stock option plan (ESOP) structure
- Significant litigation settlements related to employment

### Requires Board Notification
- Executive hires and departures (VP+)
- Significant attrition events (>3 regrettable departures in quarter)
- Material changes to people metrics (e.g., sudden drop in engagement, spike in attrition)
- Employment litigation or labor board proceedings
- Major diversity or inclusion issues
- Significant changes to compensation market positioning
- Progress against people OKRs and strategic initiatives

## Success Profile

### Traits of Successful CHROs in This Role

**The Technical Talent Magnet**
- Deep understanding of what motivates engineers and technical talent
- Respected by engineering teams for technical recruiting expertise
- Builds strong relationships with technical universities and communities
- Can speak engineering language and understand technical tradeoffs in hiring

**The Strategic People Architect**
- Thinks like a business leader first, HR practitioner second
- Designs people strategies that drive business outcomes and revenue growth
- Balances cost efficiency with investment in talent and culture
- Anticipates organizational needs 6-12 months ahead

**The Culture Steward**
- Passionate about building and protecting high-performance culture
- Intervenes quickly when culture is at risk
- Models cultural values in every interaction
- Creates scalable culture programs that don't feel bureaucratic

**The Compensation Strategist**
- Expert in designing compensation packages that attract and retain top talent
- Balances competitiveness with cost efficiency
- Creative in structuring equity to drive long-term retention
- Transparent and fair in compensation philosophy

**The Scaling Expert**
- Has pattern recognition from scaling multiple companies
- Knows when to add structure and when to stay scrappy
- Anticipates challenges before they become crises
- Builds processes that scale without creating bureaucracy

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

**Recruiting Pitfalls:**
- Over-indexing on credentials vs. actual skills and potential
- Moving too slowly on hiring and missing top candidates
- Not building diverse enough candidate pipelines
- Underestimating time-to-hire for senior technical roles
- Relying too heavily on one recruiting source or strategy

**Compensation Pitfalls:**
- Paying below market and losing talent to competitors
- Not refreshing equity for top performers (leading to retention risk)
- Creating internal equity issues through inconsistent offers
- Not benchmarking compensation regularly enough
- Over-complicating compensation structures

**Culture Pitfalls:**
- Letting culture erode during rapid growth without intervention
- Not addressing toxic behaviors from high performers quickly enough
- Creating culture programs that feel inauthentic or corporate
- Ignoring culture fit in hiring for hard-to-fill roles
- Not measuring culture health and engagement regularly

**Organizational Pitfalls:**
- Adding management layers too quickly (creating bureaucracy)
- Not adding management layers quickly enough (manager burnout)
- Creating unclear reporting relationships and role definitions
- Not investing in manager development
- Over-engineering HR processes for small company size

**Personal Pitfalls:**
- Becoming too focused on process vs. outcomes
- Not building strong enough relationship with CEO
- Trying to implement enterprise HR practices at startup stage
- Not being data-driven enough in HR decisions
- Losing touch with employees and becoming disconnected

## Onboarding & First 90 Days

### First 30 Days: Listen & Learn

**Week 1: Foundation**
- Meet 1-on-1 with CEO to align on people strategy priorities and expectations
- Meet all C-suite executives and VP-level leaders (30-60 minute meetings)
- Review current people metrics: headcount, attrition, open roles, compensation data
- Understand current recruiting pipeline and hiring needs
- Review existing HR policies, employee handbook, compliance status

**Week 2: Talent Deep-Dive**
- Interview 15-20 employees across levels and departments (sampling of organization)
- Meet with all hiring managers to understand recruiting needs and challenges
- Review compensation data and benchmark against market
- Assess current candidate pipeline and recruiting process effectiveness
- Understand technical hiring challenges and engineering team needs

**Week 3: Systems & Processes**
- Audit existing HR systems and identify gaps (HRIS, ATS, performance management)
- Review employee onboarding and offboarding processes
- Assess performance management and review processes
- Understand equity program structure and administration
- Review HR compliance status for Pakistan and international employees

**Week 4: Strategy Development**
- Synthesize findings from first 3 weeks
- Identify top 5 people priorities for next 90 days
- Draft initial people strategy presentation for CEO
- Begin building relationships with key vendors and partners
- Set 90-day goals and success metrics

### Days 31-60: Align & Plan

**Week 5: Strategic Alignment**
- Present initial people strategy to CEO and executive team
- Get alignment on priorities: recruiting, compensation, culture, systems
- Set quarterly OKRs for people function
- Define success metrics for people initiatives
